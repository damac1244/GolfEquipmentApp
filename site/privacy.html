Privacy

Bagdrop has no accounts, no sign-up and no newsletter. It asks you for nothing. Three third parties see something when you visit, and they are listed below.

Last updated 5 October 2026

What Bagdrop collects directly

Nothing. The site stores no data in your browser — no cookies of its own, no local storage — and has no login, form or mailing list. Your searches and filters are held in the page address while you browse and are never sent anywhere.

Third parties

Cloudflare (hosting)

The site is served by Cloudflare Pages. Like any web host, Cloudflare records standard request logs, which include IP addresses, for delivery and security.

Google Fonts

The page loads its typeface from Google's font servers, so Google receives the request, including your IP address.

Impact (affiliate tracking)

The site loads a tracking tag from impactcdn.com, operated by impact.com. It records that a page was viewed, sets cookies in your browser, and tags outbound links to partner sellers so that a purchase can be credited back to Bagdrop. This is how the site is intended to pay for itself. It is active on every page.

Blocking it — with a browser extension, or by refusing cookies — does not break the site. Everything works exactly the same without it.

Leaving the site

Clicking through to a seller takes you to their website, which has its own privacy policy and its own cookies. Bagdrop has no control over, and no visibility into, what happens once you are there.

Your rights

Bagdrop holds no database about you, so there is nothing for it to show, correct or delete. For data held by the parties above, those companies are the ones to approach. Questions: hello@thebagdropgolf.com.

This page describes what the site actually does, written honestly, but it has not been reviewed by a lawyer. If you are the site's operator reading this: privacy obligations differ by where your visitors live, and this is a starting point, not a compliance sign-off.
